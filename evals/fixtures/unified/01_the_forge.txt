THORNWALL KEEP — THE GREAT FORGE — SUMMER 1247

The forge fire had been burning for three days straight. Borin Ironfist, master blacksmith of Thornwall, hammered the last rivet into the breastplate and held it up to the light. Fifty-two years of smithing in these mountains had taught him to read metal the way others read faces — and this steel was afraid.

"It'll hold," he said, though his voice carried no conviction.

Elara Nightshade watched from the doorway, arms crossed, her thin frame half-hidden by shadow. She was not from Thornwall. She had arrived three weeks ago with forged papers and a story about fleeing the Southern Provinces. Borin suspected she was a spy — for whom, he couldn't say — but he needed every blade he could get.

"The garrison at Brume Pass sent a raven," Elara said. "Commander Hale is dead. Varn's Reapers took the pass at dawn."

Borin set down his hammer. Commander Hale had been his friend for thirty years. He said nothing for a long time, then: "How many survived?"

"Twelve. They're marching here. Should arrive by nightfall."

Darya, the itinerant herbalist, appeared at the forge entrance carrying a leather satchel. At thirty years old, she had already traveled more roads than most soldiers. Her green eyes swept the room, cataloging injuries before anyone spoke.

"I heard about Brume Pass," she said quietly. "I have enough nightshade extract for perhaps twenty wounds, no more. If the survivors are badly hurt, I'll need access to the eastern gardens."

Borin nodded. "Take what you need. And Darya — the Sentinels are patrolling the eastern wall. Don't let them see your satchel. They shoot herbalists on sight since the poisoning incident."

The Sentinels were Thornwall's militia, a ragged band of farmers and former soldiers who had sworn to defend the keep. They answered to no one since Commander Hale's departure, and their discipline had frayed to almost nothing.

ORACLE, the ancient mechanism embedded in the keep's foundation stone, hummed softly. Its crystalline display projected a map of the surrounding territory onto the forge wall. Red markers indicated Varn's advancing forces. Green showed Thornwall's remaining allies.

"Lord Varn's forces will reach the outer walls in approximately forty-seven hours," ORACLE announced in its flat, resonant voice. "Current garrison strength is insufficient for a sustained defense. Probability of successful resistance: fourteen percent."

"Thank you for that cheerful assessment," Borin muttered.

That night, something strange happened. Elara was seen leaving the keep by the eastern gate, alone, well past curfew. The gate sentry — a young Sentinel named Ferris — reported it to Borin the following morning.

"She said she was checking the perimeter," Ferris said. "But she went east, toward the forest. Not along the walls."

Borin filed the information away. Three days later, a Reaper patrol appeared on the eastern approach — an approach that, until now, Varn's forces had never probed. Coincidence, perhaps. Or perhaps not.


THORNWALL KEEP — THE COUNCIL CHAMBER — SUMMER 1247

The council chamber smelled of damp stone and old parchment. Borin sat at the head of the scarred oak table, Elara to his left, Darya to his right. At the far end, illuminated by a single candle, sat Maren Cole — the keep's young archivist, barely twenty, with ink-stained fingers and a memory that bordered on supernatural.

"I've found something," Maren said, pushing a crumbling document across the table. "A passage beneath the eastern wall, built during the First Siege two hundred years ago. It connects the cellars to the river cave system. Collapsed in places, but ORACLE confirmed the structural integrity of the main tunnel."

Elara leaned forward. "A way out, or a way in?"

"Both. Which is the problem."

Borin studied the map. His calloused hands traced the tunnel route with surprising delicacy. "If Varn knows about this passage—"

"He doesn't," Maren interrupted. "The records were misfiled under 'drainage infrastructure' in the third archive. I only found them because I was cataloging water damage reports from the 1190s."

"That's sixty years of misfiling," Darya observed. "Or sixty years of someone hiding it on purpose."

The room fell silent. ORACLE's display flickered, showing the tunnel in cross-section. The mechanism had existed since the keep's founding, and its knowledge of the structure was absolute — but it volunteered nothing unless asked.

"ORACLE," Borin said, "who last accessed the tunnel records?"

"Archivist Tomlin Hale, son of Commander Hale, in the year 1203. The records were reclassified at his request. No reason was documented."

Borin exchanged a look with Elara. Commander Hale's family had secrets of their own.

After the council, Borin pulled Darya aside. "I need you to watch Elara," he said in a low voice.

"You think she's the leak?"

"Someone told Varn about the eastern approach. She was out there three nights ago."

Darya frowned. "I've treated her twice since she arrived. She has scars — old ones, professional. Blade work scars on her forearms. She's trained, Borin. That doesn't make her a traitor."

"No. But it makes her capable of being one."

What neither of them knew was that ORACLE had recorded every word of their private conversation. It recorded everything. It always had.
